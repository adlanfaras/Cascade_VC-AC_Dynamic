Text explanation of Yang validation results
===========================================

The Yang validation compares the transient air-cycle model against Yang et al.
2019 test 1 data. Two experimental traces are used:

- expander outlet temperature, mapped to model variable t5_k
- expander inlet temperature, mapped to model variable t4_k

The comparison covers roughly 2.0 min to 119.7 min of experimental data. The
main purpose is to check whether the model can reproduce the measured
temperature pull-down and final temperature level during the Yang test.

Baseline two-trace validation
-----------------------------

For the baseline Yang validation run, the combined mean RMSE is 5.49 K. The
expander outlet trace has an RMSE of 7.24 K and an MAE of 4.76 K. Its mean
error is only +0.31 K, which means the outlet prediction is not strongly biased
overall, although it still has a large maximum absolute error of 30.00 K during
the transient. At the end of the test, the model outlet temperature is
107.82 K, while the experimental outlet temperature is 105.21 K, giving a
final error of +2.61 K.

The expander inlet trace is closer in RMSE, with an RMSE of 3.73 K and an MAE
of 3.21 K. Its mean error is -3.03 K, so the model generally predicts a colder
inlet than the experiment. At the end of the test, the model inlet temperature
is 133.96 K compared with the experimental value of 136.65 K, giving a final
error of -2.69 K.

Overall, the baseline result captures the final temperature level reasonably
well, especially considering that both final errors are within about 3 K. The
largest mismatch is in the transient outlet response, where the maximum error
reaches about 30 K. This indicates that the model is better at matching the
long-time operating point than the early or middle transient shape.

Counterflow-cell model calibration
----------------------------------

Several reduced regenerator model variants were fitted to the same Yang inlet
and outlet traces. The best performing case is the 6-cell counterflow model
with the initial solid temperature fitted. This fit gives:

- fitted UA = 1554.00 W/K
- fitted solid capacitance = 9960.67 J/K
- fitted initial solid temperature = 309.95 K
- outlet RMSE = 7.12 K
- inlet RMSE = 2.52 K
- mean RMSE = 4.82 K
- outlet final error = +4.35 K
- inlet final error = -0.52 K

This is the strongest validation result among the fitted variants because it
reduces the combined mean RMSE from 5.49 K in the baseline case to 4.82 K. The
inlet prediction improves clearly, and the outlet RMSE also improves slightly.
The final inlet temperature is very close to the experiment, with less than
1 K error. The final outlet temperature remains warm by about 4.35 K, so the
model still does not perfectly reproduce the cold-end final state.

The 6-cell counterflow model without fitting the initial solid temperature is
also acceptable but slightly weaker. It gives a mean RMSE of 5.22 K, with
7.80 K outlet RMSE and 2.64 K inlet RMSE. This shows that the counterflow-cell
structure itself is helpful, but allowing the initial regenerator solid
temperature to adjust improves the transient match.

The 4-cell counterflow model performs much worse, with a mean RMSE of
20.80 K. Its outlet and inlet final errors are about +27.64 K and +28.41 K,
respectively. This suggests that four cells are not enough to represent the
thermal distribution in the regenerator for this validation case.

Rejected matrix/lumped variants
-------------------------------

The lumped matrix and two-lump matrix fits should not be treated as successful
Yang validation results. Both give very large errors:

- lumped matrix mean RMSE = 80.98 K
- two-lump matrix mean RMSE = 81.19 K
- final errors exceed 90 K at the outlet and 109 K at the inlet

These results indicate that the lumped matrix structures are too simplified or
poorly conditioned for this Yang transient. They do not reproduce either the
temperature level or the transient trend with acceptable accuracy.

Conclusion
----------

The Yang validation supports using the counterflow-cell regenerator model,
especially the 6-cell version with fitted initial solid temperature. That model
matches both Yang traces with a mean RMSE of 4.82 K and keeps the final inlet
error below 1 K, while the final outlet error is about 4.35 K. The validation is
therefore good enough to show that the model can reproduce the main transient
cooling behavior and final temperature level, but it also shows a remaining
limitation in the expander outlet transient and final outlet prediction.

In short, the Yang validation is successful for the 6-cell counterflow model,
partially successful for the baseline model, and unsuccessful for the lumped
matrix variants.
